# R4 brief: long-form AI video production pipeline

As of 2026-11-01. Facts are in `raw/R4.jsonl`.

## Recommendation

Build a shot-based, keyframe-first pipeline, with automated QC gates and a repair loop.

1. **Plan.** Script, beats, then a shot list of 4 to 8 s shots. A 5-minute video is 38 to 75 shots (my arithmetic). No native clip exceeds 30 s and extensions cap at 37 to 40 s, so assembly is unavoidable (R4-001, 002, 004, 006).
2. **Lock assets.** Style bible with a 3 to 5 colour palette, plus multi-angle character and product references (R4-021, 040, 043).
3. **Keyframe each shot and approve it before spending on video** (R4-036, 041).
4. **Animate with motion-only prompts** (R4-024). Use first/last frame only for continuous action. Re-anchor every shot to the original references rather than to prior outputs (R4-044, 045).
5. **QC in two stages.** Run cheap FFmpeg and ffprobe checks first, then semantic checks (R4-049, 058).
6. **Repair, then regenerate.** A vision LLM names the defect and the segment, and the system fixes it with an edited keyframe or a text edit (R4-058).
7. **Assemble.** Normalise all clips, match colour, apply one shared finish, burn in text during assembly, duck and set loudness (R4-047, 069, 070, 072, 074 to 076).

**Master prompt.** Store these as neutral fields: subject, action, scene, camera, lighting, style, audio, exclusions, timing, references. Render per provider. Wan publishes the best vocabulary for an enum (R4-023). Dialect traps:
- Exclusions are a noun list on Veo but inline "No dialogue." on Omni and Wan (R4-014, 016, 026).
- Omni cuts between shots unless told not to (R4-017).
- Wan invents dialogue if lines are omitted (R4-026).
- Reference syntax differs by provider (R4-007, 018, 025, 028).
- Sora is gone, so build no dialect for it (R4-005).

## Key findings

- **No solid figure exists for `first_pass_accept_rate` or `overshoot_ratio`.** Published values span 3:1 to 300:1 and measure different things (R4-061 to 067). I give no planning number. The few first-pass figures, 61% to 84% (R4-059) and about 85% (R4-060), are self-reported with undefined acceptance. I could not source the repeated "90%" Seedance claim (R4-060).
- **Keyframe-first has the best evidence.** ArcFace rose from 0.260 to 0.643 in one ablation (R4-036), but that method uses face swap on 5 s clips and is the authors' own. It costs motion plausibility (R4-046) and fails on blur and extreme poses (R4-037).
- **Reference images help but do not solve identity.** On Seedance 2.0, ArcScore stayed near 0.67 to 0.70 (R4-038). Named failures are copied pose and fading detail (R4-032).
- **Identity decays with distance between appearances.** One method fell from 0.765 to 0.420 over 11 to 20 shots; a memory method held at 0.669 (R4-034). Returning characters are the main long-form risk.
- **Chaining risk is inferred.** Quality halved over 180 s in a research model (R4-045). That is model rollout, not user last-frame chaining, which no paper measures.
- **Technical QC is solved.** Defaults of 2 s need tightening for short shots (R4-049). VMAF cannot score a raw generation (R4-050).
- **Semantic QC is usable but weak on what matters most.** Vision LLMs reach rho 0.72 to 0.86 (R4-052, 053). Consistency agreement is about 60% (R4-054), temporal about 0.40 (R4-052), and CLIP 0.26 (R4-051). All are model-level or set-level correlations, not per-clip detection accuracy.
- **Do not gate identity on DINOv2** (R4-035). ArcFace is noisy and viewpoint-sensitive (R4-039, 040). Humans disagree too (alpha 0.273, R4-055).
- **Generated text remains poor.** The best tested score was 68.32 of 100 (R4-047). Veo, Kling and Seedance were not in that test. Use the OCR plus VLM method (R4-048).

## Weak-evidence claims

- All overshoot and acceptance figures are vendor, anecdotal or from old models.
- LoRA on open weights: no controlled evaluation found, only anecdote (R4-042).
- Seed reuse: no evidence it preserves identity or style (R4-071).
- Style continuity rests on production accounts and practitioner advice, not tests (R4-069, 070). Automated shot matching across AI clips: no published method found.
- Extra-limb detectors publish no precision or recall (R4-056, 057).
- Shot-length norms come from feature films, not explainers (R4-008).
- Product consistency: little direct evidence beyond vendor guidance (R4-043).

## Unknowns

- Unread primary guides: Runway Gen-4 video, Seedance 2.0 and 2.5, LTX, MiniMax, Luma (R4-027, 029, 031). Seedance's official order is the most important gap. No official Kling 4.0 guide found.
- Seed and negative prompt definitions in the Veo API reference.
- Runway's "longer videos" guidance (403).
- Per-clip QC precision and recall for any detector on current models.
- Fetched quotes passed through a summarising tool, and table rows are as it rendered them. V should spot-check these.

## What would change the answer

- **A pilot: the priority.** Run 50 to 100 representative shots across 2 or 3 models and log attempts, rejection reason and accepted seconds. This replaces every overshoot figure here. Label defects by hand at the same time to calibrate QC thresholds.
- Reliable native clips of several minutes with stable identity would reduce the need for keyframing.
- A test showing last-frame chaining holds identity over 10 or more links would simplify step 4.
- Reading the Seedance guide could change that dialect.
