# R5 brief: base model candidates (as of 2026-11-01)

## Recommendation

**Primary: Gemma 4 31B. Fallback: Qwen3.8-27B.** Both are dense, Apache 2.0 open-weight models that train on a single GPU, and vLLM lists LoRA for their architectures. GPT-OSS is usable under its licence but is not recommended as the base.

- If customers restrict Chinese-origin models, swap the fallback for **Muse Glimmer 30B** (Apache 2.0, dense, half the context; R5-067, R5-070, R5-076).
- This choice rests on licence and adapter fit, not task accuracy. No card reports a social or brand benchmark (R5-107), so run a bake-off before committing.
- Licence verdicts are research views, not legal advice.

## Key findings

**What "GPT-OSS" is.** It means OpenAI's gpt-oss-120b (116.83B) and gpt-oss-20b (20.91B), released 2025-08-05 (R5-001, R5-002, R5-013, R5-014). I found no successor, though the org page shows only 10 of 39 repos (R5-003). The safeguard variants are for safety classification only (R5-005). A blog's "June 2026 gpt-oss-8b" family contradicts the primary pages, so treat it as false (R5-004).

**GPT-OSS licence and naming.** Apache 2.0 plus a two-sentence usage policy whose only duty is to obey the law; no user threshold (R5-006 to R5-008, R5-012). Apache gives no trademark rights, so **do not use "GPT" in a product name** (R5-010). OpenAI's brand page was blocked, so its wording is unverified (R5-011).

**Why GPT-OSS is not the base.** The 16 GB headline covers inference only.
- Expert weights ship in MXFP4 and must be dequantised for training (R5-024).
- BF16 LoRA on 20b takes about 44 GB, and bfloat16 inference about 48 GB (R5-019, R5-023).
- Context is 131,072, half that of the top two (R5-015).

**Why dense wins.** TensorRT-LLM rejects quantised base weights for routed-expert MoE LoRA (R5-105), Unsloth advises against QLoRA on Gemma's MoE 26B-A4B while calling dense 31B fine (R5-041), and Axolotl's Mistral Small 4 QLoRA needs about 3x the memory (R5-084). vLLM selects adapters per request, which is the per-brand mechanism (R5-102, R5-103).

**Why Gemma over Qwen.** Gemma has stated language coverage, a lower hosted output price (BF16 routes on OpenRouter: 0.40 against 1.875), and an Axolotl 31B config (R5-040, R5-041, R5-043, R5-062). Qwen's language support is unverifiable (R5-057). Qwen has a better independent record: Artificial Analysis Index v5.0 scores it 20, while Gemma shows N/A (R5-045, R5-065). Vendor-reported figures also favour it on GPQA Diamond, 89.2 against 84.3 (R5-045, R5-064). These come from different vendors' runs. Qwen's 275 Hub adapters also suggest a broad LoRA ecosystem (R5-058).

**Dropped.**
- Too large for shared-base adapters: GLM-5.3, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Muse Spark Open (700 GB VRAM), Mistral Large 4 (R5-090, R5-094 to R5-097).
- Licence terms need counsel: Mistral Medium 3.5, with a $20M monthly revenue cap covering derivatives (R5-088, R5-089); Mistral Large 4; Muse Spark Open; Llama 4, which is also superseded (R5-098).
- Weak fine-tuning path: Nemotron 3.5 Bolt, despite a clean licence (R5-091 to R5-093).

## Unknowns

- **Top technical risk:** whether vLLM applies LoRA on FP8, NVFP4 or QAT bases (R5-104). Without it, plan BF16 serving at about 56-62 GB (R5-037, R5-054), which suggests an 80 GB GPU (my inference). Smoke-test one adapter first.
- VRAM figures for Gemma and Qwen are Unsloth's, and include system RAM. Google and Alibaba publish none (R5-036, R5-053).
- Serving throughput with many concurrent adapters is unpublished (R5-103).
- Gemma's linked prohibited-use and intended-use pages were not read (R5-031). Mistral Small 4, DeepSeek and GLM-5.3-Flash licence files were not read either (R5-081, R5-094, R5-097).
- Gemma and Qwen release dates come from secondary or listing sources (R5-044, R5-063).
- Beam and Kolibri-1 were screened on secondary sources only (R5-101).
- Artificial Analysis scores differ widely across index versions; never compare across them (R5-066).

## What would change the answer

- Qwen 4 27B ships under Apache 2.0 with better scores (R5-099). Its licence is unannounced, and Flash-Next uses a community licence (R5-100).
- The bake-off puts Qwen3.8-27B or Muse Glimmer clearly ahead of Gemma on Synquic's languages and tasks.
- vLLM testing shows LoRA failing on one dense base but not another.
- Counsel finds Gemma's linked policies bind Apache-licensed use.
- A client requirement for long threads above 131,072 tokens rules out Muse Glimmer; a US-only sourcing rule rules out Qwen.
