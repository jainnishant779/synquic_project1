# R6 brief: LoRA training, serving, risk and competition (as of 2026-11-01)

## Recommendation
Keep the shared base plus per-brand RAG as the core. Treat the per-brand LoRA as an optional voice layer that each brand must earn, by beating a few-shot prompt baseline on held-out data. RAG carries all brand facts. Train with Axolotl or Unsloth on PEFT, retrain on a schedule with immutable versions, and serve on vLLM v0.31.0 or later (SGLang is the alternative). Before any multi-tenant launch, inject a per-tenant `cache_salt` at the gateway, keep adapter loading private, and run a canary audit on every adapter. This is research, not legal advice.

## Key findings
**Tooling.** PEFT, Unsloth and Axolotl are active and support QLoRA (R6-001, 002, 003, 009). OpenAI is winding down fine-tuning (R6-008). Data guidance is vendor rule of thumb: 100 rows minimum and 1-3 epochs (R6-005, 006), against OpenAI's API floor of 10 (R6-007). Wall-clock evidence is thin: 45 h on 4x A10G for a 70B (R6-011), an unmeasured 1.25 h estimate on 4x H100 (R6-012), and 14.1 h on 4x A5000 with unclear scope (R6-013). The best measured data is Llama2-7B QLoRA at 7,631 tokens/s on an A800 and 2,151 on an RTX 4090 (R6-014, 015). Run a timing test before feeding any of these to the cost model.

**Fine-tuning vs RAG.** RAG beats fine-tuning for facts (R6-017, 019). On current events, adding fine-tuning made RAG worse, 0.875 falling to 0.830, though results on MMLU were mixed (R6-018). RAFT suggests training with retrieved documents may avoid this (R6-024). Negative results closest to our setup: small-data QLoRA had limited utility on Llama (R6-020), and zero-shot matched fine-tuned models on tweet stance (R6-025). Positive results: fine-tuning beat prompting on style transfer (R6-021), and prompting struggled to imitate implicit style (R6-022). **No study tested brand voice directly.** The case for voice LoRAs is extrapolated.

**Continuous learning.** Reports conflict. Sequential distinct tasks forget severely (R6-027, 028), while time-incremental updates showed no catastrophic forgetting in a September 2026 preprint (R6-029). I found no controlled comparison of online learning with scheduled retraining, so scheduled is the conservative choice. vLLM, SGLang and Ray docs read define no adapter version concept; llm-d's name-to-version mapping with traffic shifting is the one pattern found (R6-032). Swaps need matching target layers (R6-033).

**Serving.** TGI is in maintenance and LoRAX is archived (R6-045, 046). llama.cpp does not batch across adapters (R6-047). The vLLM default of `max_loras` = 1 is a trap (R6-034). SGLang defaults to 8 (R6-035). The active working set is the real limit: 1,000 synthetic adapters gave 884 tok/s under uniform traffic and 2,167 under skew (R6-038, 039). Mixed ranks raised P95 TTFT by 84%, so fix one rank across the fleet (R6-043). SGLang overlap loading gives about 35% on typical adapters; the "78%" blog figure applies only to adapters over 1 GB (R6-042).

**Isolation.** vLLM docs say runtime LoRA loading "should not be used in production unless it is an isolated, fully trusted environment" (R6-031). Salting is optional (R6-048). A collision fix between LoRA names and `cache_salt` shipped only in v0.31.0 (R6-049). Timing attacks are practical on real APIs (R6-051, 052). SGLang's isolation is unconfirmed (R6-053).

**Leakage.** LoRA reduces membership leakage but does not remove it: AUC 0.775 (R6-054), and 17.6% against 99.0% TPR at 1% FPR compared with full fine-tuning (R6-055). Claims that LoRA is comparable to DP are empirical, not a guarantee (R6-056). Data close to the pretraining distribution is riskier (R6-057), and brand social text may fit that (my inference). Lower rank memorises less, and generated canaries make strong audits (R6-058).

**Evaluation.** I found no public brand-voice benchmark. TweetEval is usable for sentiment, but its licences vary by subset (R6-062); SuperTweetEval lists "unknown" (R6-063). Build a private voice benchmark using the metric ensemble in R6-022. Promptfoo is now part of OpenAI but stays MIT (R6-065). Ragas shows no release since January (R6-066). DeepEval shipped 4.2.4 in September (R6-067). Langfuse joined ClickHouse (R6-068).

**Competition.** Published prices: Sprout $79-399 per seat, excluding the unpriced Listening add-on (R6-069, 070, 071); Hootsuite from $99 (R6-073); Emplifi from $1,249 (R6-082); Brand24 from $199, Mentionlytics from $49, YouScan $499 (R6-084, 085, 086). Brandwatch, Meltwater, Sprinklr, Talkwalker and enterprise tiers are unverifiable (R6-074, 076, 078, 080). Every major vendor now ships an AI agent or assistant (R6-072, 075, 077, 079, 081). Brand voice is claimed from Buffer at $5 (R6-088) to Emplifi's top tier (R6-083), mechanisms undisclosed, so "brand-trained" alone is not a differentiator.

## Unknowns
- Whether a LoRA beats few-shot or RAG for brand voice, and at what per-brand data volume.
- Measured training hours on a current small model on H100/H200, SGLang multi-LoRA tok/s, and current vLLM adapter overhead (the only figure is from 2024, R6-036, 037).
- DP utility on small per-brand datasets (R6-059).
- X terms (R6-064) and the JetBrains post (R6-061) could not be read; Sprinklr's Spring 26 release timed out.
- Quotes came through a fetch model, and some are table cells with added pipes, so they need verifier checks.

## What would change the answer
- A pilot showing LoRA clearly beats few-shot on 100 to 1,000 rows would make adapters a default. A null result would mean dropping them, which removes the LoRA serving, leakage and retrain costs.
- Flat brand traffic would raise GPU cost per brand, because skew drives the throughput gap.
- A successful canary extraction or a confirmed cross-tenant cache leak would justify dedicated replicas for each brand, which undercuts the shared-GPU economics.
- If counsel blocks training on X data, per-brand training sets shrink towards each brand's own content.
